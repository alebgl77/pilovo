# Changelog

## Unreleased

- Adds a bilingual website (English at the root, French under `/fr/`) built by `npm run build:site` from `site/` and deployed to GitHub Pages by `.github/workflows/pages.yml`: statically pre-rendered pages with self-hosted fonts and no third-party requests or cookies, FAQ pages generated from `docs/faq.md` and `docs/faq.fr.md`, structured data, a sitemap and `llms.txt`, checked by `npm run test:site`. No extension behavior changes.
- Adds an animated interface tour to the English and French READMEs (`docs/media/pilovo-tour-{en,fr}.gif`): the actual UI with synthetic data, unreleased screens labeled as such.
- Adds keyboard commands. **Go to the next item to review** (suggested `Alt+Shift+J`) focuses the earliest unacknowledged attention through the same revalidation as **Open session**, provided the fresh sample still shows that attention. When revalidation or activation fails, it moves on to the next candidate (each initially selected candidate at most once, never looping). When nothing needs review it opens the side panel, in the command's window or the last focused browser window; after every candidate failed it also tries to, best effort. **Mark all items to review as seen** has no default key; assign it at `chrome://extensions/shortcuts`.
- Adds a **Mark all as seen** panel action when two or more items need review. It acknowledges locally only the attention identities the panel displayed and confirms through a refreshed snapshot. A discreet hint shows the configured next-item shortcut and hides when none is assigned.
- Records a monotonic `raisedAt` time for each attention identity to order attention, and adds the `acknowledgeAllWebActivity` RPC. Commands are not permissions; no page data, persistence or permission changes.
- Shows elapsed durations: **Working for 4 min** on working rows and **Available for 2 min**, **Input requested for…** or **Error visible for…** on items to review, seen or not (French: **En cours depuis…**, **Réponse disponible depuis…**). Observations carry a monotonic `stateSince`; the panel formats it to the minute and refreshes at minute boundaries only while a duration is shown. Durations are ephemeral: never persisted, exported or sent, and they restart when the worker loses evidence (no recognized signal, a failed sample, expired evidence, navigation, worker restart), so they can be shorter than the real time. A panel-only interruption (failed refresh, unavailable service, access change in progress) hides them until fresh data confirms the same observation.
- Differentiates the toolbar badge. It still counts live, unacknowledged attention, now on the color of the most urgent reason: red for a visible error, amber-brown for an input request, olive for a response available. With nothing to review but live work, it shows `…` on neutral gray instead of a count; seen attention, idle and unknown sessions never light it. Every background carries white text with a contrast of at least 4.5:1, checked by a unit test. The icon's tooltip gives the same summary in text, in the language chosen in Pilovo's settings rather than the browser's, for example **Pilovo — 3 to review, including 1 visible error · 1 working** (French: **Pilovo — 3 à vérifier, dont 1 erreur visible · 1 en cours**), or **Pilovo** when nothing counts. Badge and tooltip contain only counts and fixed reason words, never titles, URLs, labels or content. Unchanged values make no `chrome.action` call. Expiry lowers or clears the badge through the background watchdog and alarm, still best effort; revocation and tab closure clear their rows' contribution on Chrome's events, and a worker restart repaints it hidden before any fresh evidence. No permission or persisted data changes.
- Identifies sessions stably. Rows read **Claude · session 3** with a number the service worker assigns per service when it first observes the tab (the smallest one free) and keeps while that tab stays open on that service, so another tab closing or the page navigating no longer renumbers them; numbering restarts with the worker. A secondary line gives the tab's position, **Tab 4**, plus **other window** when it is not in the panel's window (French: **Onglet 4 · autre fenêtre**). A pencil button (**Name this session** / **Nommer cette session**) turns the heading into a field for a name of up to 40 characters; the row then reads **Contract review — Claude · session 3**, keeping its number. Enter or leaving the field applies the name, Escape cancels, an empty name clears it; control and bidi override characters are refused, and, as for focus and seen, only a live observation can be named. Names are typed by the user and kept only in the worker's memory for that tab: kept across navigation in the tab and shown only on the service where they were given, cleared on tab close, removal of that service's access or a worker restart, and never persisted, exported, sent or shown on the badge, its tooltip or any notification. Observations gain `sessionNumber`, `tabIndex` and `label`; adds the strict `labelWebActivity` RPC and a `tabs.onMoved` listener. No permission change; tab titles and URLs are still never shown.
- Adds an optional automatic seen, off by default. In **Settings › Activity**, **Mark as seen automatically** (**Marquer comme vu automatiquement**) offers Off, 5 s, 15 s or 30 s. When set, an item to review is acknowledged locally once its own tab has stayed the active tab of Chrome's focused window for that delay; Pilovo's full-page view, another tab or window, or another application in front does not count. A tab or window switch, Chrome losing focus, navigation, tab closure, revocation, a new attention identity, a manual acknowledgment or a setting change stops the wait, while tab activity in a background window does not; just before acknowledging, the service worker asks Chrome again and re-checks the same attention, still unacknowledged and live. The row then shows its usual **Seen** state and the badge lowers; keyboard focus on the row's **Mark as seen** or on **Mark all as seen** moves to the row actions instead of being dropped. The activity footer states the delay while it is enabled. It reads only tab and window IDs and their active and focused flags, in memory, through `tabs.onActivated`, `windows.onFocusChanged`, `windows.getLastFocused` and `tabs.query`; no permission is added, and Chrome is not queried while this setting is off and no service is checked for system notifications (which query the same way, below). Settings gain `autoAcknowledgeSeconds` (strict `0 | 5 | 15 | 30` in `updateSettings`); settings saved by earlier versions read as off without a migration, and the export's settings carry the value. Seen still never means verification, approval or task success.
- Adds optional OS notifications per service, off by default. In **Settings › Activity**, **System notifications** (**Notifications système**) offers one checkbox per service, **Claude** and **ChatGPT**. Checking the first asks Chrome for the optional `notifications` permission, from that click only (the service worker never requests it); a refusal leaves the setting unchanged and is announced, and unchecking the last service removes the permission. When Chrome no longer grants it (removed elsewhere), both services read as off with an explanation and can be checked again; the browser preview disables them with the reason. The service worker shows one notification per newly raised attention identity on a checked service, with the permission, when its tab is not the active tab of the focused window, and never for attention raised before the service was checked or twice for the same signal re-raised after its evidence lapsed with nothing else observed in between. It contains only the service name, the reason and the session number in the app's language (**ChatGPT** · *Intervention demandée · session 2* / *Input requested · session 2*), never a session name, tab title, URL or content. At most one per tab (a newer attention replaces it) and 16 live. When only its evidence lapses (expired, failed or unrecognized samples, as in a throttled background tab), a notification stays up to 35 s and, if the same reason is raised again in that tab meanwhile, stands for that new identity without a second notification. Clicking one clears it and opens the session through the usual revalidation (first sampling the tab again if its evidence had lapsed), never marking it seen; seen (manual, mark all or automatic), replacement, evidence lapsed beyond 35 s, navigation, tab closure, revocation or unchecking the service clears it, and a restarted worker clears notifications a previous one left. Unchecking the last service clears them before the page removes the permission (the settings reply waits for the clears); once the permission is removed elsewhere Chrome refuses to clear them, so the worker forgets them and clears any left on screen when the permission is granted again. An activity alarm that wakes the worker, or a site access grant, now starts observing only after the stored settings apply. The manifest gains `optional_permissions: ["notifications"]` (required permissions and hosts unchanged; `package:check` and its publication unit tests now require exactly this optional permission and refuse any other); settings gain `notifyProviders` (strict list of distinct services, default empty, read as empty from settings saved earlier, carried in the export); the legacy `notifications` flag is untouched. The operating system may keep notifications in its notification center.
- `check:publication` refuses raw bidirectional control characters (U+202A–U+202E, U+2066–U+2069) in published text; source writes them as escapes.
- Fixes the expiry watchdog, which could run a fraction of a millisecond before the earliest deadline (the clock is fractional and timers truncate their delay) and then not re-arm, leaving the badge lit until the next alarm or panel read. It now rounds the delay up and re-arms when nothing has expired yet.
- Fixes **Open session** and the next-item shortcut, which could fail with **This session could not be opened** on an unchanged tab (present in 0.4.0 for **Open session**): routine page hints arriving during focus's one-second wait before its fresh sample, or a routine sample already in flight, took that sample's slot. While a focus is pending, hints now wait for its own fresh sample, and a sample already in flight is awaited instead of failing focus; it is never used as proof.
- Documentation: the English and French READMEs open with a definition of Pilovo, a facts table, a table of states and their panel labels, a no-affiliation statement and short FAQs, and move unreleased-feature details to the installation guides; adds `docs/faq.md`, `docs/faq.fr.md`, a French installation guide (`docs/installation.fr.md`), a French permissions table and `CITATION.cff`, and links the v0.4.0 prerelease directly. No product behavior changes.

## 0.4.0 — Pilovo Preview

- Introduces Pilovo, the native P identity and the promise “Keep your AI in view.”
- Adds experimental local ChatGPT/Claude web-status observation with separate optional site grants and a bundled isolated observer.
- Distinguishes witnessed work, response-ready attention after debounce, explicit recognized attention, and unknown/expired evidence; adds focus, seen acknowledgment and a last-observed toolbar badge.
- Keeps activity metadata ephemeral and manual preparations separate; preserves existing database/protocol identifiers and historical receipts.
- Updates EN/FR installation, privacy, compatibility and release gates. Claude's quota-blocked stale completion announcer is documented as a counterexample; authenticated extension E2E is not claimed.
- Retains deterministic packaging and license/privacy checks, with a required observer bundle and exact permission/CSP checks.

This is a pilot scope, not a store release or universal agent manager. Final validation and publication remain separate gates in [RELEASE](docs/RELEASE.md). Older Briefoak archives remain unchanged.

## 0.3.0 — Briefoak

- Preserves manual copy/send/status behavior, optional site permissions, IndexedDB identity and historical receipt provenance.

This entry describes the release scope; [RELEASE](docs/RELEASE.md) records validation. Store publication is not included.
